GRASP: Reptile Environment Monitoring System

Goal

Your goal is to investigate and design a reptile environment monitoring system.

The system needs to help zoo keepers monitor conditions in a reptile enclosure, especially temperature and light, so they can make decisions that support reptile wellbeing.

You are not building the full enclosure.

You are designing and explaining how the monitoring system would work.

Role

You are working as a technologies designer.

Your job is to think like someone who designs useful systems for real people. You need to understand the problem, identify the user’s needs, investigate environmental data, and explain how a micro:bit system could help.

Audience

Your audience is the zoo keeper team.

They need a system that gives them useful information about the reptile enclosure, especially when they are busy, away from the enclosure, or checking conditions over time.

Situation

Reptiles rely on their environment to stay healthy. They cannot control their body temperature in the same way humans can.

Zoo keepers need to know:

  • Is the enclosure too hot?
  • Is it too cold?
  • Is there enough light?
  • Are the conditions changing over time?
  • Could the reptile be at risk if nobody checks the enclosure soon?

A monitoring system could help by using sensors to collect information and then displaying or communicating a warning.

Product

You will create a Design Investigation that explains your proposed system.

Your work should include:

  • user need analysis
  • environmental conditions, such as temperature and light
  • inputs, processing and outputs
  • a labelled system diagram
  • an explanation of safe and unsafe conditions
  • ethical and wellbeing considerations
  • evidence from practical micro:bit investigation

Standards for success

A strong response will:

  • clearly identify the user, zoo keepers
  • explain why reptiles need suitable temperature and light
  • explain why monitoring over time matters
  • select suitable micro:bit hardware, such as temperature and light sensors
  • describe thresholds, rules or conditions the system checks
  • explain what the system displays or communicates
  • use technical terms such as input, processing, output, sensor and threshold
  • justify design decisions by linking them to animal wellbeing
